# The Art of Becoming Dangerously Self-Educated (complete plan in 2 minutes)

## [00:00] Three Techniques for Self-Education

Becoming dangerously self-educated requires only three simple techniques and has nothing to do with innate talent or reading speed.

*   **The Explain It Simple Test**: Based on Richard Feynman's rule that if you cannot explain something simply, you do not understand it yet. The method involves teaching a concept aloud as if explaining it to a ten-year-old listener.[1] When you encounter sticking points during this process, those are identified as gaps in understanding rather than memory failures.
The process requires going back and fixing only the specific spot where confusion occurs before attempting another explanation of that same idea.

*   **The Lattice Work Move**: Popularized by Charlie Munger, who avoided collecting facts without connecting them. The key is to ask yourself every time you learn something: what does this connect to in your existing knowledge base?
This question transforms isolated data points into an interconnected structure, ensuring information is useful rather than a messy pile of disconnected trivia.
